After an action runs, the Witness checks it against an observation. The level says which observation.

| Level | What it compares the report with | Who reaches it |
|---|---|---|
| <LevelBadge level="L0" /> | The tool's own response: it returned success, and its output matched the tool's schema | Every connector, and every proxy-mode edge |
| <LevelBadge level="L1" /> | A read-back from the target after the action: the record exists and the named fields match | REST, Postgres (the row, by `xmin`), GitHub, Slack, Stripe test mode, Notion |
| <LevelBadge level="L2" /> | The row changes Postgres committed in the action's transaction, read from its own change log | Postgres, with `wal_level = logical` |

A policy rule says the level it needs (`verify: L2`). The deed claims one level and one verdict.

## The three verdicts

- <VerdictChip verdict="VERIFIED" level="L2" /> The observation at that level found no difference, and nothing at any level disagreed.
- <VerdictChip verdict="MISMATCH" level="L2" /> Some level found a difference: for example, Postgres changed 301 rows where the agent reported 1. A violation anywhere gives MISMATCH, at the highest level that found one.
- <VerdictChip verdict="UNVERIFIABLE" level="L1" /> No level disagreed, but none at or above the required level could observe cleanly: a read-back timed out, the change log was behind, or Deedglass couldn't tell whether the tool ran. The reasons name the gap.

## How one verdict is chosen

Each check produces reasons, each a pass, a gap or a violation. Any violation gives MISMATCH. Otherwise, a level with at least one pass and no gap, at or above the required level, gives VERIFIED at the highest such level. Otherwise the verdict is UNVERIFIABLE. A gap is never turned into a MISMATCH, and nothing short of a clean pass becomes VERIFIED. One function makes this decision, and it is tested over every combination of results.

## Late evidence

L2 can arrive after the deed was sealed (the change log was behind). The late verdict seals an **amendment deed**; the first deed never changes.

## What it doesn't do

<Limits>
- L1 trusts the target's answer. A target that answers consistently but falsely can't be caught by reading it back.
- L2 sees only the watched tables in Postgres's change log: not effects outside Postgres, not unlogged or temporary tables, not other databases.
- L2 expectations exist for simple statements. A JOIN, `UPDATE … FROM`, a CTE or `MERGE` gets the gap `footprint_unknown`, so it can't be VERIFIED at L2.
</Limits>
