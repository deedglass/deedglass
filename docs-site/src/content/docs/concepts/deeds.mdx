A deed is the signed record of one action: who asked, what was decided, what ran, and the verdict.

## What a deed holds

- The agent, on-behalf-of and session; the tool, connector and mode; digests of the request and the signed intent.
- The policy version, decision and rule; the approval, with who countersigned.
- The execution: status, timings, a digest of the result, the error code.
- The witness: level, verdict, method and a digest of the detail.
- The cost, and the previous deed's hash.

Payloads never go inside a deed, only their SHA-256 digests. The body is canonical JSON (RFC 8785), and the seal is an Ed25519 signature over `"deedglass/deed/v1\n" + body_hash`.

## The chain

Each org's deeds are numbered from 1 with no gaps. Each deed carries the hash of the one before it, and the database refuses a second deed with the same predecessor, so the chain can't fork. Seal keys are published at `/.well-known/deedglass/keys.json`.

```sh test title="Terminal"
$ deedglass ledger check
✓ Chain intact · … deeds · seq 1 → … · …
```

## Check deeds offline

Anyone with the public keys can check a chain without Deedglass running: `deedglass verify`, the TypeScript or Python verifier, or this page's checker. See [Verify deeds as an auditor](/guides/verify-as-an-auditor/).

## What it doesn't do

<Limits>
- A deed shows that a request with a given digest was handled. You need the original request to match it.
- Seal keys are per deployment: one set signs every org's deeds. Per-org seal keys come later.
- A deed is signed by your Deedglass instance. It's as trustworthy as that instance and its seal key.
</Limits>
